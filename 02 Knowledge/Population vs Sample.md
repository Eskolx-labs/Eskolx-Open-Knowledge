# Population vs Sample

## Definition
A **population** is the entire set of individuals or items you want to draw conclusions about. A **sample** is a subset of that population that you actually observe or measure.

## Intuition
You almost never can measure everyone, so you look at a representative slice and reason about the whole from it.

## Why it matters
Nearly every statistic you compute (a mean, a proportion, a model estimate) is calculated from a sample but is meant to say something about the population. Confusing the two leads to wrong conclusions.

## How it works
Define the population precisely, draw a sample with a method that avoids bias (for example, random sampling), compute sample statistics, then use inference to estimate population parameters together with a stated uncertainty.

## Example
To learn the average height of all adults in a city (the population), you measure 500 randomly chosen adults (the sample) and use their average as an estimate of the population average.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating a sample statistic as if it were the exact population value.
- Drawing a non-representative (biased) sample and generalizing from it.
