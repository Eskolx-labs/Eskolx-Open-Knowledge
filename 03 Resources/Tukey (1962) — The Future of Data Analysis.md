# Tukey (1962) — The Future of Data Analysis

## What it is

John W. Tukey, "The Future of Data Analysis," *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics*, 33(1), 1962, pp. 1–67. [doi:10.1214/aoms/1177704711](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177704711).

The paper argues that data analysis is a science in its own right. It is not a branch of mathematics, and it is not a service performed after the mathematics is done. Tukey separates exploratory analysis from confirmatory analysis. He puts computing inside the method. He also names the loop that this lab runs every week: look at the data, form a question, build a tool, check the result, learn something, and look again.

## Why we recommend it

It states why Eskolx works in this form. We do not read a method and then write a function as an exercise. We read the source, build the method, check it against a reference, and explain it. That is Tukey's argument in practice.

The paper also explains why week 1 starts with the data contract. If data analysis is a science, the contract is an instrument. A function that silently drops a row or coerces a string is a bad instrument, however clean the formula looks.

## How to use it

- Read the introduction, the sections on exploratory and confirmatory analysis, and the sections on the role of computing. Skip the detailed examples on the first pass.
- Bring one sentence to the review that changed how you think about your own block.
- Ask one question about your function. Is this work exploratory or confirmatory? The two have different standards. An exploratory summary can be informal. A confirmatory test must state its error rate and its assumptions before it sees the data.
- Do not quote the paper as authority for a design choice. Use it to explain why the choice matters.
